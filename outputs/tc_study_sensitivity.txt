Where radar noise enters
  White radar bearing noise (sigma_theta = 3.491e-04 rad per sample, flat to the 62.8 rad/s Nyquist of the 20 Hz radar) reaching the loop as a reference error e_ref:
  - feedback-only path: theta = T e_ref, so noise inside the bandwidth (|T| ~ 1 up to 5.7 rad/s) passes untouched: the loop can only remove the part above it.
    fraction of noise power passed (integral of |T|^2 over 0..wN divided by wN): 0.148 (amplitude 0.385);  cross-range at 2000 m if the raw bearing drove the loop: 0.269 m (raw 0.698 m)
  - with the exact feed-forward added (our 2-DOF design) the nominal map from the reference to theta is 1 (G * G^-1 = 1): the loop does NOT attenuate reference noise at all;
    the only low-pass left is the reference generator (triple pole p = 20 rad/s): power fraction 0.187, |F| at wN = 0.0279 (-31.1 dB)
  - T and F both have bandwidth far above the LOS maneuver band (<= ~0.3 rad/s, see tc_study_sampling), so neither shapes the in-band noise: it must be removed by the estimator before the loop.
    That is the Kalman filter: its steady-state cross-range error at 2000 m is 0.42 m against 0.70 m raw, and 2.52 m after a 0.5 s blackout (outputs/error_budget.txt).
  Disturbance (torque) rejection: |S| < 0.1 below 0.666 rad/s; at the plant resonance |S| = 1.09, i.e. a disturbance there is amplified by 9 % (the peak Ms = 1.21 is the worst case, at 51.6 rad/s).
  Voltage cost of noise: the feedback path adds |KS| (peak 4227 V/rad); the feed-forward path costs |G^-1 F| at wN = 589 V/rad (G^-1 = s (a2 s^2 + a1 s + a0)/gn).
