1. Radar rate against Nyquist
   radar period 50 ms -> 20 Hz, Nyquist 10.0 Hz = 62.83 rad/s.  Plant resonance 55.94 rad/s = 8.90 Hz is 89 % of that Nyquist; loop crossover 4.10 rad/s is 6.5 %.

2. LOS-rate content of the worst-case target (v = 150 m/s, a = 20 m/s^2, r = 800 m)
   LOS rate    |theta_dot|  <= v/r = 0.1875 rad/s (10.74 deg/s)
   LOS accel   |theta_ddot| <= a/r + v^2/r^2 = 0.0602 rad/s^2  (tangential acceleration plus 2 v_r v_t / r^2 at 45 deg to the line of sight)
   equivalent frequency: a sinusoidal LOS rate of amplitude 0.1875 rad/s with acceleration amplitude 0.0602 rad/s^2 oscillates at w = theta_ddot/theta_dot = 0.321 rad/s = 0.051 Hz
   -> 0.0051 of the radar Nyquist; 392 radar samples per cycle (Nyquist asks for 2).
   motion between two radar samples: rate x T = 9.38 mrad = 7.5 m of cross-range at 800 m (this is what the estimator extrapolates; it is large against the 2 m budget, small against the sampling theorem).
   fastest maneuver family simulated (simulation/tc_independent_generator.m, bang-bang acceleration reversing every 1 s): content up to ~2 pi/(1 s) = 6.3 rad/s = 10 % of the Nyquist - still below it, but not by a large margin; that family is a stress case, not part of the handout envelope.
   Radar noise is white and sampled at the radar instants: there is no signal above Nyquist to alias (the target signal sits ~196x below it), so no anti-alias filter is needed; the noise itself is what the Kalman filter smooths.

   At 20 Hz the resonance (55.9 rad/s) is 89 % of the controller Nyquist (62.8 rad/s): a 20 Hz controller cannot shape it, only gain-stabilise it; the continuous-equivalent model is
   reliable only where the crossover is well below Nyquist (crossover/Nyquist = 0.065 at 20 Hz; the comment in tc_design_report.m says the two models agree below about Nyquist/5), which holds for the crossover but NOT near the resonance.
